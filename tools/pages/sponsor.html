Sponsors back drivers who make it easy to say yes. Fill this in once, and get a proposal and an email you can tailor for each company.

About you

Facts, not adjectives. Numbers beat "passionate and dedicated".

Under 18? Ask a parent or guardian to send the email and be the contact. Sponsors expect it, and it keeps you safe.

What the sponsor gets

The biggest mistake is only asking. Show what they get back.

Who you're asking

One proposal per company. Name a person and say why them.

Where to start looking
  • Businesses near your home or your track: garages, builders, trades, gyms, cafés
  • Your parents' and family's employers and suppliers
  • Companies whose customers are drivers or car fans
  • Karting and motorsport brands for products rather than money

    The mistakes that lose sponsors
    
    
    Sending the same email to everyone
    "Dear Sir/Madam" goes straight in the bin. Use their name, mention their business, and say why you picked them.
    
    
    Only asking, never offering
    A sponsor is buying something. List exactly what they get: logo places, posts, videos, guest days and reports.
    
    
    No numbers
    Give followers, views, how many people see you race and how many meetings you do. Small honest numbers beat vague big claims.
    
    
    No clear ask
    Say how much, or which product, and what it pays for. "Any support would be great" is easy to ignore.
    
    
    Writing an essay
    Keep the email under 200 words and the proposal to one page. Put the details in the attachment.
    
    
    Bad photos, or none
    Send two or three sharp, well-lit photos of you and the kart. Mock up their logo on the kart if you can.
    
    
    Going quiet after the deal
    Send a report after every round, tag them every time, and say thank you. That's how a sponsor renews next year.
    
    
    Giving up after one email
    Follow up politely after a week, then once more. Many sponsors say yes on the second or third message.
    
    
    Starting too big
    Local businesses say yes far more often than big brands. Start local, build a track record, then go bigger.
